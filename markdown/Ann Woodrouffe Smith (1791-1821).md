---
title: "Ann Woodrouffe Smith (1791-1821)"
---

- Born 1791, daughter of Thomas Woodrouffe (later Thomas Woodrouffe Smith) and Anne Reynolds.
- Had a younger sister Maria, who (on [3 September 1833 at the Friends Meeting House, London](http://www.old-liverpool.co.uk/Oct1833.html)) married [George Head Head (d.1876)](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Head_Head).
- Married (in Wandsworth, London, on 29 August 1811) [John Barton Senior (1789-1852)](John%20Barton%20Senior%20%281789-1852%29.md).
- Died October 1821, aged ~30, without issue? (See below).

There is constant disagreement about the spelling of her name: Anne or Ann? Woodrouffe or Woodruffe? And are Woodruffe and Smith hyphenated together? There doesn't seem to be any consistency. For example, in a [letter](_Writings%20of%20John%20Barton%20Senior.md#letter-from-john-barton-senior-to-his-children-april-1850) written by her husband John he writes it as 'Woodruffe Smith', and the same spelling is used by his son John in his [memoir](_Memoirs%20of%20John%20Barton.md), but in her marriage record it is written 'Woodrouffe Smith', so that is the spelling followed here.

There has also been some disagreement about her issue. [\*Smith 1878](_Smith%201878.md) records "*his first wife was Ann Smith, by whom he had issue a dau. — Ann Barton, who mar. Rev. William Harvey, s.p.*". But in the letter above John writes: "*As she left no children I felt doubly desolate*", which is pretty definitive. It never made much sense why John would name his later daughter [Anne](Anne%20Barton%20%281831-1921%29.md) if his first wife had a daughter by that name who survived to marriage age!

Her father was friends with the poet Robert Southey, according to a [letter from Southey](_Bernard%20Barton%20the%20Quaker%20Poet%20Correspondence.md#robert-southey-to-bernard-barton-october-1820-p154) to [Bernard Barton the Quaker poet (1784-1849)](Bernard%20Barton%20the%20Quaker%20poet%20%281784-1849%29.md): "*It has accidentally come to my knowledge that a [brother](John%20Barton%20Senior%20%281789-1852%29.md) of yours is married to the daughter of my worthy and respected friend, **Mr. Woodruffe Smith**. When you have an opportunity, it would oblige me if you would recall me to her remembrance, by assuring her that I have not forgotten the kindness which I so often experienced at her father's house.*"

# Marriage

The following is Public Record Office reference RG 6/541:\
![john barton anne woodrouffe smith 1811 SMALL.jpg](../files/john%20barton%20anne%20woodrouffe%20smith%201811%20SMALL.jpg "john barton anne woodrouffe smith 1811 SMALL.jpg")

"*On the <u>twenty ninth</u> Day of the <u>eighth</u> Month, One Thousand <u>Eight</u> Hundred and <u>eleven</u>... <u>John Barton</u> of <u>Chichester in the County of Sussex, Merchant</u>.... Son of <u>John Barton, late</u>.... of <u>Hertford deceased</u>.... and <u>Elizabeth</u>.... his Wife, and <u>Ann Woodrouffe Smith</u>, Daughter of <u>Thomas Woofrouffe Smith</u> of <u>Stockwell in the County of Surry deceased</u>.... and <u>Ann</u>... his Wife, took each other in Marriage in a publick Assembly of the people called Quakers, in <u>their meeting house at Wandsworth in the County of Surry</u> in the presence of us*

<u>*Tho.s Reynolds of Wallington, Surry, Bleacher*</u>\
<u>*Geo. Harrison, Wandsworth*</u>\
<u>*Sam.l West of Billiter Lane, London, Warehousekeeper*</u>

*This Marriage was solemnized between us,*\
<u>*John Barton*</u>\
<u>*Ann Woodrouffe Smith*</u>"
