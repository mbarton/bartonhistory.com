---
title: "Hartsop Hall"
---

A working farm owned by the National Trust (see [www.hartsophallcottages.com](http://www.hartsophallcottages.com)).

[\*Curwen 1932 pp256-277](_Curwen%201932.md#256-277) describes it thus:\
"<u>*Hartsop Hall*</u>*.**The former residence of the **Bartons of [Ormside](Ormside%20Hall.md)**. It is a little old building say of the 15th century consisting of two blocks at right angles to one another. There is nothing remaining of any note about it."*\
Hartsop is also briefly mentioned in [\*Nicolson et al 1777](_Nicolson%20et%20al%201777.md) [p407](_Nicolson%20et%20al%201777.md#407), which may be Curwen's primary source.

There is a [coat of arms](Coats%20of%20Arms.md) on the church in [Barton hamlet](Barton%20%28hamlet%20%26%20parish%29.md) which [has been associated](_Transactions%20CWAAS%20v5%201905.md#88) with the owners of Hartsop (it does have harts on it), and Hartsop Hall lies within the [parish of Barton](Barton%20%28hamlet%20%26%20parish%29.md). It may indeed be that the parish is named for the [Bartons of Ormside](Ormside%20Hall.md#Bartons), and that its peculiarly stretched boundary [owes its origins to the distribution of lands owned by this family](Ormside%20Hall.md#hartsop-hall--barton-parish-connections).
