---
title: "Mary Irving (1804-1890)"
---

- Born ~29 May 1804, daughter of [Thomas Irving (b.1780)](Thomas%20Irving%20%28b.1780%29.md) and [Elizabeth Dodgson (b.1782)](Elizabeth%20Dodgson%20%28b.1782%29.md).
- Married, on 21 November 1822, [William Barton (1785-1846)](William%20Barton%20%281785-1846%29.md)
- Lived at No. 1, [The Crescent, Carlisle](https://maps.google.co.uk/maps?q=The+Crescent,+Carlisle&hl=en&ll=54.892011,-2.932513&spn=0,0.01929&sll=54.887667,-2.937298&sspn=0.028535,0.077162&gl=uk&z=16&layer=c&cbll=54.891937,-2.93257&panoid=ooS39ph4E8hpyyvEn7-iPA&cbp=12,146.24,,0,-12.81), as her daughter [Elizabeth](Elizabeth%20Barton%20%281822-1909%29.md) and son [Robert](Robert%20Barton%20%281828-1917%29.md) did after her.

[\*Penfold](_Penfold.md)'s interview of her son [Robert Barton (1828-1917)](Robert%20Barton%20%281828-1917%29.md) says: "*his **Mother** an Irving of **Middlemoor** & a cousin of that old [Bewcastle](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bewcastle) family the Dodgsons of **Roanstree**, Thomas being the most prominent."*

An excellent history of the Dodgson family, written by Robert Dodgson in 1843, is online [here](http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~rutledge/DodgsonManuscript.htm), and mentions Mary and some of her Dodgson ancestry.

[\*Penfold](_Penfold.md) later writes that her son Robert "*was brought to Carlisle in swaddling clothes when 11 months old on the 11th of November 1829 - & lived in a house now the shop of Messrs Fairbairn*[?] *in English Street; the rental of which in those days was £70 per year. Owing to the close proximity of the gaol*[?] *- where culprits were hung in public, his **mother** insisted on their removal to the Crescent to the House now occupied by Mr. Kekwick*[?] *- on the failure of Forster-Bank in 1836 - Gale the owner & builder of the Crescent & who built & lived in the house so long occupied by Mr Barton - was forced to sell & William Barton Robert's father purchased the house & ground to Dents'*[?] *shop - of the large garden & extensive buildings* ???? *unto*[?] *Mary Sheil*[?] *- for between three & four thousand £s & where they have resided ever since 1837, the year of [Queen Victoria](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria)'s ascension to the throne a period of 80 years.*\
\\\
*His **mother** was born in Lowther Street in a house latterly occupied by Mr Bawson*[?] *V.S. & now demolished - she inheriting that property the Three Crowns Inn & the Three Crowns public house in English Street - also property in New Bank Lane* [Bank Street?] *where a theatre which was run by "Daddy baby*[?]*" & which she closed on the advice of Johnny*[?] *Fawcett*[?] *- from her uncle Robert Jame's "an old Carlisle landmark".*

The location of the **Three Crowns Inn**, determined from [this old map](http://www.abebooks.co.uk/Plan-valuable-freehold-estates-situate-Carlisle/971462346/bd), is shown below. Note that the [Dodgson family history](http://www.rootsweb.ancestry.com/~rutledge/DodgsonManuscript.htm) claims that Mary's parents also kept (before or after this?) the Queens Head Inn at Wigton.
