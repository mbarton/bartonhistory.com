# Welcome!

UPDATE: Recently some image links broke on the site. I think I have repaired them all now, but let me know if I missed any.

My name is [David B. H. Barton](Living%20connections.md) and this '[wiki](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiki)' is an online encyclopaedia of my family history. My family has been interested in its own history for generations, and we've amassed a huge archive of material. The lion's share of this information has been painstakingly gathered, over several decades, by my father [Nicholas J. Barton](Living%20connections.md#Nicholas%20Barton). My job, as I see it, is to digitize it, organize it, and share it with the handful of people who might be interested in it. Still, there is some original research of mine on here as well. I don't have much time to add more at the moment though.

The name Barton is Anglo-Saxon in origin, meaning either 'barley farm' or 'lands of the manor or meadow', depending on [who you ask](The%20Barton%20name.md). It's quite a common place-name in England and there are probably many different Barton families hailing from [different places called Barton](The%20Barton%20name.md#places). There's even a [Barton Historical Society](http://bartonsite.org/) to help keep track of them all, and they run a [DNA project](DNA.md) too. If you are a U.S. Barton looking for clues to your origin you'll probably have better luck with their site than mine.

My particular Bartons can be traced back to [Ivegill](Ivegill.md) in Cumbria (yellow on the map below) in the 18th century, and evidently they were there nearly two [centuries](_Dalston%20parish%20records.md) before that. Ivegill is only 11 miles NNW of a tiny hamlet called [Barton](Barton%20%28hamlet%20%26%20parish%29.md) (red on the map) which was once a sizeable parish, and it's possible that the Ivegill Bartons were previously from there. However there were also wealthy Bartons living not far away in [Ormside Hall](Ormside%20Hall.md) (blue) and possibly also [Hartsop Hall](Hartsop%20Hall.md) (green), and a [document exists](Ormside%20Hall.md#Farflung) which links the Ormside Bartons to lands in [Yorkshire](Whenby%2C%20Yorkshire.md) and Northumbria, and to Barton families in Lancashire, Yorkshire and Essex. It's all very intriguing. In fact is it possible that the [hamlet and parish of Barton](Barton%20%28hamlet%20%26%20parish%29.md) were named after the Barton family of Ormside and Hartsop Halls [who owned property throughout it](Ormside%20Hall.md#hartsop-hall--barton-parish-connections)?

In 2011 I learned of a [second line](William%20Barton%20%281700-1763%29.md) of Bartons [supposedly](_Penfold.md) coming from [Ivegill](Ivegill.md). Early members of this family used many of the same first names as early members of my own branch, and like my branch they have links to [Bartons Farm in Thomas Close](Bartons%20Farm%2C%20Thomas%20Close.md), but we have yet to find really solid proof connecting them. A modern American descendant once [claimed](_Maupin%201980.md) that it is an old family tradition that they are descendants of the Scottish 'pirate'/privateer [Sir Andrew Barton (1466-1511)](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Barton_(privateer)), a connection that would be fun to prove. While no direct ancestry can be demonstrated, there may indeed be some relation: one of Sir Andrew's ancestors, [Robert de Barton](Robert%20de%20Barton%2C%20King%27s%20clerk%20%2813th-14thC%29.md), a royal official of [Edward I](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England), moved to Carlisle from [Yorkshire](Whenby%2C%20Yorkshire.md) and might perhaps have been the ancestor of many Cumbrian Bartons.

In my own line, there have been - down the centuries - [many](Bernard%20Barton%20of%20Carlisle%20%281728-1773%29.md) [interesting](John%20Barton%20the%20Elder%20%281754-1789%29.md) [Bartons](John%20Barton%20Senior%20%281789-1852%29.md), with quite a few [famous friends](Famous%20connections.md). But my ancestors have also married into many other interesting families: [Broadbents](Esther%20Broadbent%20%281873-1959%29.md), [Elliotts](Emily%20Elliott%20%281839-1924%29.md), [Dougans](Emily%20Dougan%20%281806-1877%29.md), [Boileaus](Alicia%20Boileau%20%281779-1851%29.md), [Rickmans](Josephina%20Rickman%20%281808-1892%29.md) and [Hornes](Elizabeth%20Horne%20%281760-1833%29.md), for instance. Thanks to my [Boileau ancestor](Alicia%20Boileau%20%281779-1851%29.md), I can even trace a descent from [William the Conqueror](Royal%20ancestry.md) and beyond. Many other families also join the tree - not necessarily my direct relatives but equally interesting nonetheless: the [Fiennes](Famous%20connections.md#Fiennes), [Youngs](Sir%20William%20Mackworth%20Young%20%28b.1840%29.md), [Beamishes](Arthur%20Beamish.md), [Moules](Rev.%20Handley%20Moule%20%281841-1920%29.md), [Hacks](Maria%20Hack%20%281777-1844%29.md), [Venns](Famous%20connections.md#James%20Stephen), [Thorntons](Susan%20Thornton%20%281875-1958%29.md), and [Grenfells](Katherine%20Charlotte%20Grenfell%20%281842-1906%29.md) for instance. This makes 'Barton History' a rather poor name for the site, but a better one has yet to suggest itself.

For those wondering about the wheel in the logo at the top-left of the page: it represents the 'horizontal flax wheel' invented by early patriarch [Bernard Barton of Carlisle (1728-1773)](Bernard%20Barton%20of%20Carlisle%20%281728-1773%29.md). Oddly enough a very similar symbol appears on older inscriptions, one on a [house](Ive%20Bank%2C%20Ivegill.md), one on a [bell](High%20Head%20Chapel.md#Bell), both next to the letters IB, thought to be the initials of Bernard's [yeoman](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeoman) grandfather [John Barton (d.1720)](John%20Barton%20%28d.1720%29.md).

If you don't know where to start... well, pride of place in this collection should probably go to the memoirs of my great great grandmother [Emily Elliott (1839-1924)](Emily%20Elliott%20%281839-1924%29.md). These are now fully annotated with hyperlinks, maps and illustrations. Take a look [here](_Memoirs%20of%20Emily%20Elliott.md).

Do please [get in touch](Living%20connections.md) if you have any questions or information to add, or simply to introduce yourself. It's always interesting to meet new distant relatives or connected family researchers, and if you'll let me I'd be pleased to add you to my [Living Connections](Living%20connections.md) page.
