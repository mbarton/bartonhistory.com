[Ivegill](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivegill) is a tiny village in Cumbria, situated on a stream (or [gill](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gill_(stream))) called the [Ive](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ive). It's also been known variously as Ive-Gill, Hyvegill, Hivegill, or High Head / Highead (these latter probably due to its proximity to [High Head Castle](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_head_castle)).

# Sites of interest

[Ive Bank](Ive%20Bank%2C%20Ivegill.md), a house believed once to have been owned by [John Barton (d.1720)](John%20Barton%20%28d.1720%29.md), sits next to the river at the south end of the village, next to an ancient stone packhorse bridge (see below). Across the road is a farm called [The Grange](The%20Grange%2C%20Ivegill.md), which belonged to the Bewley family. About 1 mile to the west of Ivegill are [High Head Castle](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_head_castle) and [High Head Chapel](High%20Head%20Chapel.md) which some Bartons helped to build and run.

# Parish allegiance

Ivegill lies at the [boundary between three parishes](Parishes%20of%20Cumberland%20and%20Westmorland.md): [Dalston](_Dalston%20parish%20records.md), Hesket-in-the-Forest, and an outlying area of the St Marys in Carlisle parish. But it's also very close to Castle Sowerby parish, Skelton parish, and Hutton-in-the-Forest parish. It may also be that parish boundaries have changed over the years, or have otherwise been unclear:\
In the [will of John Barton (d.1720)](John%20Barton%20%28d.1720%29.md#will), he writes: "*I John Barton of Ivegill Head in the parish* *of **Heskett***".\
In the [will of his son John Barton of Ivegill (d.1747)](John%20Barton%20of%20Ivegill%20%28d.1747%29.md#will), he writes: "*I John Barton of Hive-gill in the Parish of **Dalston**.*"\
Likewise, in the [will of the latter's wife Ann (d.1748)](Ann%20%28d.1748%29.md#will), she writes "*I Anne Barton of Hive-gill in* *the Parish of **Dalston***" despite declaring her property to be in Thomas Close (which is in Hutton-in-the-Forest parish).
