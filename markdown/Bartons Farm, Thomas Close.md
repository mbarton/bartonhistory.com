According to Andrew Humphries, most of the current buildings date to the 19th century, but there is one inscription that certainly seems older:

![Bartons Farm Thomas Close inscription.JPG](../files/Bartons%20Farm%20Thomas%20Close%20inscription.JPG "Bartons Farm Thomas Close inscription.JPG")\
It appears to read "17 BB 14": possibly meaning "B. Barton 1714"?

[\*Maupin 1980](_Maupin%201980.md) suggests that the ancestors of [Robert Barton (1747-1795)](Robert%20Barton%20%281747-1795%29.md) were from "*Ivegill and Thomasclose*", although Richard Hargreaves disputes that lineage and has Robert as the son of [William Barton (1700-1763)](William%20Barton%20%281700-1763%29.md), finding no reference to Thomas Close.

[\*Penfold](_Penfold.md), however, which is an interview with [Robert Barton (1828-1917)](Robert%20Barton%20%281828-1917%29.md), says:

*"He inherited on the death of his [father](William%20Barton%20%281785-1846%29.md) **Thomas Close** - which his [grandfather](Robert%20Barton%20%281747-1795%29.md) had purchased & left to his [Second Son - Robert's father](William%20Barton%20%281785-1846%29.md) - the elder son inheriting the [Ivegill](Ivegill.md) Estate. Thomas Close will ever be remembered in the political history of Cumberland as the place* *where an accident was supposed to have happened* [in 1839] *to the Great [Brougham](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Brougham,_1st_Baron_Brougham_and_Vaux) & Mr Leader M.P. on their way from Highhead Castle to Thackwood nook* *- where they were going*[?] *to partake of tea with William Blamire & his sister Jane - but instead of going to Thackwood they turned up at Penrith*[?] *- the news of the accident leaked out. As Brougham wished it - & he had the satisfaction of reading his own obituary - & the scathing attacks on him in the leading daily London papers.\**\
\\\
*Robert farmed **Thomas Close** for six years (which he left on purchasing Barrockstown in 1852) & having only had two tenants in the long period of 65 years on Thomas Close & where some of the old chains*[?] *of Rork's*[?] *invention may still be seen.*

\*This incident is described, somewhat differently, in <u>**Memoirs of the Right Honourable Henry Lord Brougham (1840)**, by J. Harwood</u> (online [here](http://books.google.co.uk/books?id=Yxfykesq5kwC)). The subject of these stories was [Henry Peter Brougham, 1st Baron Brougham and Vaux (1778–1868)](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Brougham,_1st_Baron_Brougham_and_Vaux). His father was [Henry Brougham (1742-1810)](http://thepeerage.com/p16743.htm#i167424), who had lived at **Scales Hall**, the property just to the west of Bartons Farm. Lord Brougham's great x4 grandfather [Henry Brougham (d.1622)](http://thepeerage.com/p18152.htm#i181518) had been the first to live there.

Lord Brougham's great great uncle [Peter Brougham](http://thepeerage.com/p18155.htm#i181546) had married [Elizabeth Richmond](http://thepeerage.com/p18155.htm#i181547), daughter of [Christopher Richmond](http://thepeerage.com/p18155.htm#i181548) of [High Head Castle](High%20Head%20Chapel.md), and his wife Isabella Miller; in Isabella's [1737 will](_Jackson%201892.md#pp130-143) - that was witnessed by a John Barton, possibly [John Barton of Ivegill (d.1747)](John%20Barton%20of%20Ivegill%20%28d.1747%29.md)? - mentions her grandson [Henry Richmond Brougham](http://thepeerage.com/p18155.htm#i181549) (not then 21; d.1749 without issue).
